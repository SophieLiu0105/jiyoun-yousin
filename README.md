季风有信 · Where is Home

A digital archival exhibition on Lyon Choi (Cai Li'an, 蔡里安, 1912–2005), a Chinese-Vietnamese sculptor, watercolourist, composer and educator whose life traced the cross-cultural currents of overseas Chinese communities across Saigon, Hong Kong, Guangzhouwan (Zhanjiang), Wuhan and Guangzhou in the first half of the 20th century.

About the Exhibition

Born in Saigon to parents from Dongguan, Guangdong, Lyon Choi graduated from the École des Beaux-Arts de l'Indochine in 1934. Forced into exile after his teacher's anti-colonial activism, he moved to Hong Kong, where he taught art and joined anti-Japanese cultural fronts. During the Pacific War he took refuge in the French concession of Guangzhouwan, producing watercolours and patriotic songs including the West Wind Songbook (西风歌集). After 1949 he returned to China, becoming one of the founding faculty of the sculpture department at the Guangzhou Academy of Fine Arts. His 1945 work *Worker's Head* was the first piece by a GAFA teacher or student ever selected for the National Art Exhibition of China.

This digital exhibition is adapted from the 2023 physical show *"Where is Home: Lyon Choi and the Reality Narration of Overseas Chinese in Southeast Asia in the First Half of the 20th Century" held at the Guangzhou Academy of Fine Arts Art Museum (curated by Chen Xiaoyang and He Xiaote). It reconstructs the artist's peripatetic journey through the port cities of the monsoon coast, and situates his quiet, humanist practice within a larger, under-told history of diasporic Chinese artists whose lives shaped modern Chinese art education from the south.
